The one-line thesis

Ola Electric lit the fuse on India’s EV-scooter boom, hit ~25% share — and then collapsed to under 6% in a year. The cause wasn’t the product idea; it was service and quality — 10,000+ complaints, a CCPA probe, and a founder admitting service broke brand trust. The companion app rates 2.44★, the lowest in this portfolio. This teardown is a case study in how the after-sales experience can unmake a market leader.

01 · The product

A great scooter, a broken experience

Buy a scooter → pair the companion app → use it to book service, track status and manage the vehicle. The hardware won early; the software and service around it lost.

Who

Early EV adopters — who became the loudest detractors when service failed.

How it earns

Vehicle sales (now opening to dealers) plus service and parts.

Why it faltered

A DTC model that scaled sales faster than it scaled service capacity — the gap became the crisis.

02 · Business & unit economics

The cost of poor service

The economics here aren’t a clean per-unit margin — they’re the cost of poor service: warranty, lost referrals, and brand damage that shows up directly in the market-share collapse.

Service debt compounds

Every unresolved complaint is warranty cost plus a lost referral — and in a word-of-mouth category, that compounds into lost share.

Trust is the real asset

The founder’s own admission: service failures damaged brand trust. Rebuilding it is the only path back.

Analyst read

The north star isn’t units sold — it’s service resolution × owner advocacy. Until the after-sales experience and the app work, every sale risks creating another detractor. Fix service, and the funnel can heal.

04 · Market

Pioneer to cautionary tale

Ola created the category’s momentum, then handed share to rivals who simply served customers better. The market punished an experience gap, not a product one.

Play Store rating (recent sample)
600-review sample · count in tooltip

The rating is the story

At 2.44★ — 348 of 600 one-star — the companion app is the single clearest signal of the crisis: service 1.39★, app/tech 1.77★, support 1.39★. “OLA fraud” recurs.

So what

The lesson is blunt: a great product can’t survive a broken experience. Ola’s only path back is service reliability, a working app, and trust — which is why the voice of customer, not the spec sheet, is the whole analysis.

The read

This is the lowest-rated app in the portfolio — 2.44★: service quality 34% avg 1.39★, app/tech 39% (1.77★), support 1.39★, pricing 1.10★ (“OLA fraud”). A textbook service crisis — the app and after-sales experience destroyed the brand trust the market-share collapse reflects.

Beyond the Play Store · external signal

Beyond the 600 Play reviews this is cited press (Reddit blocks automated access). The reviewed app is the vehicle companion app (com.olaelectric.companion).

  • A documented collapse — E2W market share fell from 24.8% (Jan 2025) to under 6% (Jan 2026); the CCPA is probing 10,000+ after-sales complaints; the founder acknowledged service hurt brand trust.
  • The fix attempt — Ola is overhauling service and opening its network to traditional dealers — a reversal of the DTC model that scaled sales ahead of service.

What good looks like

The leak is after purchase — at service. App pairing and booking fail, resolution is slow, and the owner becomes a detractor. Fix the service SLA and the app, and the funnel — and the share — can start to heal.

The risks
