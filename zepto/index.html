The one-line thesis

Zepto created the 10-minute category and still has the product love — a 4.75★ rating, higher than any peer. But it runs a distant second to Blinkit and carries the heaviest burn in the sector: FY26 net loss ~₹5,905 Cr even as revenue doubled. That burn has repriced it — 2026 pre-IPO talks are at ~$3–4.5B, down from $7B in Oct 2025 — and the IPO is deferred. The whole story is one question: can it turn category love into profitable frequency before the runway runs out? That's an analytics problem.

01 · The product

Speed first — then a widening wedge

Core is the same 10-minute dark-store model. Zepto's edge is product velocity: it layered on Zepto Café (food & beverage in minutes), Super Saver (cheaper, next-slot delivery) and Zepto Pass (membership) to lift frequency and basket beyond pure grocery top-ups.

Who

Young, urban, metro-first users — the earliest adopters of instant delivery, high intent and high frequency, but also the most promotion-sensitive.

How it earns

Take rate on GOV + delivery/handling fees + a growing ad business + Zepto Pass subscription. Café adds a higher-margin, higher-frequency basket.

The wedge

Café & Super Saver widen the use-cases a single dense store can serve — more orders per store without more real estate.

02 · Business & unit economics

Love is cheap; profit is the test

Revenue doubled again in FY26, but the net loss widened to ~₹5,905 Cr — the classic growth-stage shape. With no deep-pocketed parent and a deferred IPO, capital discipline is existential, and investors are now judging the path to contribution-positive orders.

Burn is the story

FY26 net loss ₹5,905 Cr, up ~26% YoY. But the per-order math is improving: adjusted-EBITDA loss narrowed to ₹78.75/order (FY26) from ₹136 a year earlier — the trend that has to continue.

No parent safety net

Unlike Blinkit (inside Eternal), Zepto funds expansion from VC and a deferred IPO. That makes contribution margin per order the metric that governs survival, not just growth.

Analyst read

The lever set is identical to any q-commerce player — AOV, order frequency, fulfilment cost — but the stakes are higher because there's no balance sheet to hide behind. The first job is a trustworthy per-order contribution model so every pricing, Pass and Café decision is made on margin, not momentum.

What good looks like

Healthy q-commerce retention flattens into a high plateau — the grocery habit, once formed, recurs. The analyst's job is to shorten time-to-habit: push a new user to their 3rd order fast, because that's where the curve stops decaying — and that's exactly what Pass and Café are for.

First 30 · 60 · 90 days as your analyst

  • Days 0–30 — one source of truth: the activation→habit funnel and a cohort-retention dashboard wired to events, so growth and finance read the same numbers before the IPO roadshow.
  • Days 30–60 — find the biggest retention leak (likely time-to-3rd-order) and ship the Pass and Super-Saver experiments with margin guardrails.
  • Days 60–90 — a per-order contribution-margin model by store & cohort — the single most important number for a company that must prove profit, not just growth.
06½ · Extra analyst reads

Three more things the data says

Beyond the headlines — the second-order reads a hiring manager actually wants.

Growth without expansion

FY26 revenue roughly doubled while the store count grew only ~11% (1,029→1,139). Growth is density- and throughput-led, not store-led — efficient, but it also means the easy store-expansion runway is thinner than Blinkit's.

The loss ratio is improving

Net loss was ~42% of revenue in FY25 and fell to ~26% in FY26 — even though the absolute loss grew. The trajectory, not the headline ₹5,905 Cr, is the real story for an IPO.

Per-order burn nearly halved

Adjusted-EBITDA loss per order dropped from ₹136 (FY25) to ₹78.75 (FY26). At that slope, contribution breakeven is in sight — and sustaining the slope is the entire pre-IPO thesis.

07 · Moat & risk

What protects it, what threatens it

The moat

  • Category-creator brand love — the highest rating in the sector (4.75★).
  • Product velocity — Café, Pass & Super Saver widen use-cases per store.
  • Founder-led speed — fast iteration and dense metro coverage.

The risks

  • Heaviest burn, no parent — survival depends on capital discipline + IPO access.
  • A bigger, backed leader — Blinkit's lead & Eternal balance sheet; plus Instamart.
  • Regulatory / labour — dark-store FDI scrutiny and gig-worker cost pressure.

Comparability note: Zepto's ₹3,367 Cr is a company-level net loss; Blinkit discloses segment-level adjusted EBITDA. The two are not an apples-to-apples comparison — always normalise the denominator before ranking "who burns more."
