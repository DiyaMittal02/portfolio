The one-line thesis

Stable Money’s insight is that most Indians want safety, not equity — and FDs are clunky to open across banks. By making fixed deposits bookable in-app without a new savings account, it built ₹5,000 Cr+ of AUM. It’s well-liked; the levers are reliability (loading, card transactions) and rate/charge transparency — because this is people’s savings, trust is the product.

01 · The product

FDs without the friction

Compare rates → book a fixed deposit (or bond, or digital gold) in a few taps, no new savings account. Stable Money is the distribution and trust layer on top of partner banks and issuers.

Who

Risk-averse savers — Bharat, first-time investors who trust FDs and gold over markets.

How it earns

A commission/spread from partner banks and issuers on deposits booked.

Why it wins

Removes the account-opening friction and surfaces the best rates — convenience + trust in one place.

02 · Business & unit economics

Thin take, compounds on renewal

Stable Money earns a commission per deposit, thin per user, so economics compound only with renewal at maturity and repeat deposits — which reliability and transparent rates protect.

Illustrative contribution per investorillustrative
Shape from commission less infra, support & marketing; not Stable Money’s P&L

Renewal is the engine

A saver who re-books at maturity is near-free AUM. Maturity nudges and trust are what drive it.

Reliability is trust

A failed booking or a stuck card transaction, with people’s money, is a trust event — not just a bug.

Analyst read

The north star is AUM retained × renewal, not installs. Make money-movement reliable and rates transparent, and savers re-book — the only way a thin commission compounds.

04 · Market

The fixed-income distribution layer

Stable Money competes less with other apps and more with bank branches and inertia. Its edge is convenience + trusted rates for a saver who fears equity.

07 · Moat & risk

What protects it, what threatens it

The moat

  • Convenience — FDs without a new savings account.
  • Trust — safety-first product for Bharat.
  • Distribution — best rates across partners.

The risks

  • Reliability — failed money-movement breaks trust.
  • Thin take — needs renewal to compound.
  • Regulation — fixed-income distribution scrutiny.
