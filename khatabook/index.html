The one-line thesis

Khatabook won by making the merchant khata digital, free and simple — 50M+ downloads. The hard turn now is monetising a free utility without breaking it: a Pro subscription paywalls once-free basics, ads fill the app, and a lending arm chases margin. The reviews are blunt — the ledger is still loved, but the monetisation layer, especially lending, is eroding the trust that made it sticky. That tension is the analyst’s whole job here.

01 · The product

A paper ledger, in your pocket

Record credit/debit per customer, send payment reminders by SMS/WhatsApp, and collect via UPI/QR — the daily bookkeeping a kirana or small business used to do on paper.

Who

Small merchants — kirana, retail, distributors — across Tier-2/3 India, in 13 languages.

How it earns

A Pro subscription, a payments take (UPI/QR), and a growing lending business (interest + partner commission).

Why it wins

Daily habit plus switching cost — a merchant’s whole receivables ledger and customer list live inside it.

02 · Business & unit economics

Monetising a free habit

The ledger was free to win scale; profitability now rides three streams — subscription, payments and lending — each of which touches the trust that made the ledger sticky.

Illustrative revenue mixillustrative
Shape from subscription + payments + lending; not Khatabook’s P&L

Lending is highest-margin, highest-trust-risk

A bad loan experience — spam calls, data-sharing, a hit to a credit score — costs the ledger relationship too.

The paywall taxes loyalty

Putting once-free basics (entry limits, export) behind Pro angers the long-tenure merchants who are the base.

Analyst read

The north star is retained active merchants, not paid conversions. Monetise around the free ledger — payments, optional Pro value, carefully-run lending — not through breaking it.

The read

Khatabook won 50M+ merchants by being a free, simple ledger. Chasing profitability, it now monetises three ways at once — a Pro paywall on once-free basics, ads, and a lending arm — and all three generate its angriest reviews. Lending is the sharpest: it's the highest-margin bet (the ₹1,000 Cr-AUM story), but the complaints — spam calls, data shared with third parties, a hit to the credit score, no loan statement or closing — damage the ledger brand too, because it's the same app holding the merchant's whole khata. And the export/entry paywall doesn't tax new users; it taxes the 5-year loyalists who are the base. The core utility is loved; the way it's being monetised is quietly spending the trust that made it sticky.

How I'd measure it — monetisation-driven churn as a first-class metric

The instrumentation I'd stand up in week one

  • Split cohort retention by monetisation event — did the merchant hit the paywall, see heavy ads, or take a loan? Track each group's D30 vs a clean cohort; the gaps are the real cost of each lever.
  • Loan-complaint rate and data-consent opt-outs as first-class metrics — lending revenue that costs the ledger relationship is negative-sum.
  • Loyal-merchant churn (tenure > 1 year) after a paywall change — the base is the moat, so churning it to convert a few is a bad trade.
  • Guardrail: the Play Store rating and the “scam / fake loan” share of reviews — brand damage here raises the cost of every future install.

07 · Moat & risk

What protects it, what threatens it

The moat

  • Daily habit — the receivables ledger lives in the app.
  • Switching cost — a merchant’s whole khata and customer data.
  • Distribution — 50M+ downloads, 13 languages.

The risks

  • Monetisation backlash — paywall + lending + ads churn the loyal base.
  • Lending brand damage — spam, data-sharing, credit-score complaints.
  • Cooled category — SME-bookkeeping growth has slowed.
