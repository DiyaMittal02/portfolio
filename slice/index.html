The one-line thesis

slice has crossed from fintech app to bank. The product itself is genuinely loved — 62% of reviews are pure praise — so the risk the reviews expose isn't the app, it's service at scale: the one recurring complaint is support (“the app is good but customer support is very bad — callback only, at their convenience”). As a bank that now holds deposits, support becomes a trust-and-compliance surface, not a cost line. The gap between a loved app and a trusted bank is service.

01 · The product

A credit-first app, now a full bank

UPI payments, a credit line and card, and now savings and deposits under an RBI small-finance-bank licence. slice owns both the relationship and, now, the balance sheet.

Who

Young, mobile-first Indians who started on slice’s credit/UPI product and now bank with it.

How it earns

Interest and spread on lending and deposits, plus card and payments economics — classic bank unit economics now.

Why it wins

A loved app funnel plus an RBI licence: it controls acquisition and the balance sheet.

02 · Business & unit economics

From loss-making fintech to profitable bank

FY26 was slice’s first full profitable year — ₹48.4 Cr PAT against a ₹216.7 Cr FY25 loss — on ₹5,765 Cr deposits and a ₹5,098 Cr loan book. A bank funds lending with cheap deposits, so winning and keeping depositors is the whole game.

Deposits change the game

A bank funds lending with cheap deposits — but deposits demand trust and service the app never had to give.

Support is now a regulated surface

For a bank, a support failure isn’t just a bad review — it can escalate to the RBI / banking ombudsman.

Analyst read

The north star is trusted active bank relationships — a user who both transacts and keeps a deposit. Protect it with bank-grade service, because that is what a deposit is really buying.

04 · Market

The neobank-to-bank race

slice is rare — a fintech that actually got a bank licence, unlike Jupiter or Fi on partner banks. The edge is owning the balance sheet; the test is delivering banking-grade service.

Play Store rating (recent sample)
600-review sample · count in tooltip

Loved app, bank-grade expectations

At 4.27★ with 62% pure praise, the product is clearly loved — the anger is narrow: support (2.07★) and a few payment-failure and charge issues. As a bank, that narrow gap matters more.

So what

The moat is being both the app people love and the bank that holds their money. slice’s job is to make service match the licence — because a deposit relationship is held by trust, not features.

Feature ideas · from the reviews

What I’d ship to make the app better

The product is loved; service is the gap between app and bank. Each idea protects the trusted relationship a deposit requires. Each is pulled from a real complaint theme above, with a product-analyst’s rough impact/effort call — opinion, clearly labelled.

1. Add real human support with call-back SLAs

From the reviews

Top complaint support 2.07★ — “app good, support bad; callback at their convenience.”

Expected impact

Deposits demand bank-grade service; protects the balance-sheet relationship.

Impact: HighEffort: Med

2. Make statements and charge clarity bank-grade

From the reviews

“Statement should separate interest / transactions”; charge confusion.

Expected impact

Fewer disputes and 1-star reviews on money clarity.

Impact: MedEffort: Low

3. Harden payment / UPI reliability

From the reviews

Payment-failure cluster (2.69★) — stuck or failed transactions on a money app.

Expected impact

A failed payment on a bank is a trust event.

Impact: HighEffort: Med

4. Proactively communicate through the bank transition

From the reviews

Users confused by card / limit / bank changes post-merger.

Expected impact

Reduces churn and uncertainty during the licence transition.

Impact: MedEffort: Low

07 · Moat & risk

What protects it, what threatens it

The moat

  • Bank licence — owns the balance sheet, not a partner’s.
  • Loved funnel — a 62%-pure-praise app feeding the bank.
  • Profitable — FY26 first full-year PAT.

The risks

  • Service gap — app-grade support vs bank-grade expectations.
  • Inherited loan book — North East SFB’s legacy book needed clean-up.
  • Regulated now — support failures can become RBI complaints.
  • Competition — Jupiter, Fi and super-apps on the same users.
