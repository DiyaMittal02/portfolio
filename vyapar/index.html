The one-line thesis

Vyapar is infrastructure for the small Indian business — the tool a shopkeeper actually bills, tracks stock and files GST on. That makes it sticky and high-LTV. The reviews show the tension of monetising it: the tool itself is trusted, but tightening the free tier and a sales-first support experience are souring the very long-tenure businesses that are the base. For a daily, business-critical tool, that's the churn risk that matters.

01 · The product

The small business, digitised end-to-end

Create GST invoices, track inventory, record payments and cash flow, send reminders, bill offline — on mobile and desktop. More than a ledger: a full billing-and-accounting stack.

Who

Small Indian businesses — retail, wholesale, services — that need GST-compliant billing and simple accounting.

How it earns

A free mobile app feeding paid premium/desktop subscriptions and value-added services.

Why it wins

It runs the business's day — invoices, stock, GST — so switching cost is high and usage is daily.

02 · Business & unit economics

High-LTV, but loss-making while it scales

FY25 revenue ₹77 Cr (+40%) against a ₹63 Cr net loss — Vyapar is spending to grow the paid base. A sticky, daily tool means a retained paying business compounds; a churned one is expensive to replace.

The free tier is the funnel — and the fuse

Tightening it (an invoice cap) converts some merchants and alienates others; on a daily tool, the alienated ones write the angriest reviews.

Support is a renewal lever, not a cost

On a business-critical tool, a merchant who can’t get help at tax time doesn’t renew — the reviews say support is sales-first.

Analyst read

The north star is retained paying businesses. Monetise the free tier carefully and make support service-first — both protect the renewal that a high-LTV, daily tool is built on.

The read

Vyapar is a system of record — a business bills, tracks stock and files GST on it every day, which makes the base sticky and high-LTV. That's exactly why the two pains are dangerous: a tightening free tier (an invoice cap where there used to be none) and sales-first support (attentive before the sale, silent after) both hit the long-tenure, paying merchants hardest — the 11-year customer, not the trial user. On a daily, business-critical tool, those are the people whose renewal funds the company, and they're the ones writing the 1-stars. The core product is loved; the way it's monetised and supported is spending the trust that makes it a system of record.

How I'd measure it — renewal, split by tenure

The instrumentation I'd stand up in week one

  • Renewal rate split by tenure and by whether the merchant hit the new free cap — the long-tenure, cap-hit cohort is the risk.
  • Support resolution + CSAT at renewal time, not just at sale time — a failure at tax season is a non-renewal.
  • 1-star share attributable to pricing / undisclosed IAP as a monitored metric — it's a direct install-conversion drag.
  • Guardrail: functionality bug-close time (GST, printing) — the core job has to just work for a system of record.

07 · Moat & risk

What protects it, what threatens it

The moat

  • System of record — the business bills, tracks stock and files GST on it daily.
  • Switching cost — years of invoices and accounts live inside.
  • Breadth — full billing+accounting, mobile and desktop.

The risks

  • Monetisation squeeze — a tightening free tier churns the loyal base.
  • Sales-first support — neglect at renewal time loses high-LTV users.
  • Loss-making — ₹63 Cr FY25 loss; pressure to monetise harder.
  • Crowded market — Khatabook, myBillBook, Zoho on the same ground.
